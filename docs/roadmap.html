Ntụgharị Roadmap

Ntụgharị started as a bridge for heritage learners who understand Igbo but freeze when it is time to speak. The plan is to grow it into one place to learn the language, practise speaking it, and understand the people, places and history it comes from.

Phases, in order

Each phase builds on the one before it. Status shows where each one stands today.

  1. 1

    Fix the foundations

    Code done · deploy pending
    • Tutor moved to a current AI model; it had been giving canned replies since its old model was retired.
    • Chat history fixed so the tutor sees the conversation correctly.
    • AI tutor, speaking feedback and voice transcription now need a free account, so the public can't run up the bill.
    • A visible notice when the tutor falls back to simple replies.
    • Corrected Igbo in the fallback phrases, fixed font loading and type errors.
  2. 2

    Voice: test ElevenLabs, cache audio

    Ready to test
    • ElevenLabs Scribe added to the speech-to-text chain, with a switch to compare providers on the same recording.
    • Every generated clip is saved once and replayed from storage, so each phrase is paid for one time.
    • Next: compare voices on tone pairs such as ákwà (cloth), àkwá (egg), àkwà (bed).
    • Goal: one warm, consistent narrator, ideally a native speaker's voice cloned with their consent.
  3. 3

    Proverbs · Ilu

    Next
    • A proverb of the day with its literal translation, its meaning, and when people use it.
    • Audio for every proverb, and a “finish the proverb” practice mode.
    • Grouped by theme: patience, family, community, wisdom, work.

    Cheap to build on the existing content editor and close to the heart of the app. In Chinua Achebe's words, proverbs are “the palm-oil with which words are eaten.”

  4. 4

    Map & dialects

    Planned
    • An interactive map of Igbo-speaking areas, from the core states out to parts of Delta and Rivers.
    • Standard Igbo (Igbo Izugbe) stays the default; each word can carry its dialect variants, tied to a place on the map.
    • Tap a region to hear how a word or greeting changes there, and read about the towns, festivals and history linked to it.
  5. 5

    History

    Planned
    • Nri, one of the oldest kingdoms in the region, and the Igbo-Ukwu bronzes of the 9th–10th centuries.
    • The Aro Confederacy and its trade networks.
    • Colonial rule and the 1929 Women's War (Ọgụ Ụmụnwaanyị).
    • The Nigerian Civil War and Biafra, 1967–1970: survivors' accounts and historians' work, carefully sourced.
    • Igbo communities around the world today.

    Each story links to places on the map and to the words it uses.

  6. 6

    Music & films

    Later
    • Guides to listening, from highlife greats like Osita Osadebe and Oliver De Coque to today's artists such as Flavour and Phyno.
    • Films in Igbo and films about Igbo life, with where to watch them.
    • Vocabulary pulled from lyrics and scenes, so listening becomes practice.

    We link to official sources and never host other people's music or films.

How dialects will be shown

Learners start with Standard Igbo, so they can be understood everywhere. Dialect variants sit beside it, as part of the language's richness. The groupings below are a working starting point to refine with speakers from each area.

Some communities in Delta and Rivers speak closely related languages and do not all identify as Igbo. The map will say so plainly rather than draw one border around everyone.

How we build it

Native speakers check the Igbo

Every fixed phrase, proverb and audio clip is reviewed by a fluent speaker before it ships.

Sources are cited

History and culture pages name where their facts come from, and credit the collections we draw on.

Shame-free by design

Corrections model the right form gently. Speaking practice never mocks a learner.

Hard history handled with care

Biafra is still painful for many families. We give context and many voices, and stay out of present-day politics.

Voices used with consent

Any cloned or recorded voice comes from someone who agreed to it and is credited.

One content system

Proverbs, songs, films and history share one flexible content type, so new sections don't need a rebuild.

Where help would make the biggest difference

  • Fluent speakers to review phrases and record audio.
  • Speakers from different areas to supply dialect words and greetings.
  • Historians, teachers and elders to shape the history pages.
  • Collectors of proverbs, songs and family stories.
